section-2-1-nav: Context
section-2-1-title: Context
# section-2-1-description-1-title:
section-2-1-description-1: The existing SoleSavy platform had been built for a different stage of the company. It was a Wordpress site managed by an external agency — functional enough when the membership was smaller, but increasingly unable to support the product direction SoleSavy wanted to move in. Third-party integrations were difficult. Custom flows were impossible. The platform had become a ceiling.
# section-2-1-description-2-title:
section-2-1-description-2: But the platform wasn't the only problem. SoleSavy's onboarding was effectively nonexistent. After signing up, new members landed in a community with no clear guidance on what to do, where to go, or how to get value. The product had a steep learning curve — getting a shoe on release day required understanding how to use SoleSavy's tools, and nobody was hand-holding new members through that. Churn in the early weeks was the predictable result.

section-3-1-nav: Research
section-3-1-title: Research
# section-3-1-description-1-title:
section-3-1-description-1: The research for this project was grounded in what SoleSavy already knew about its members — and what it could see in the data about where new members were falling off.
section-3-1-description-2-title: Churn patterns
section-3-1-description-2: The internal data pointed clearly at the early weeks as the highest-risk period. Members who didn't engage with SoleSavy's tools quickly tended not to engage at all. The problem wasn't that the tools were bad — it was that too many members never found them.
section-3-1-description-3-title: Member interviews
section-3-1-description-3: We talked to members who had been around long enough to get value from SoleSavy about what clicked for them and when. The consistent theme was a specific moment — usually a first successful shoe purchase — where the value became undeniable. Getting new members to that moment faster was the design goal.
section-3-1-description-4-title: Platform audit
section-3-1-description-4: We mapped every touchpoint in the existing Wordpress experience to understand what was worth preserving versus what needed to be replaced outright. Most of it needed to go.

section-4-1-nav: Ideation
section-4-1-title: Ideation
# section-4-1-description-1-title:
section-4-1-description-1: With the decision made to move to a custom platform, we started by rebuilding the user journey from the top. Not just the sign-up screen — the full arc from discovery, to first purchase, to long-term retention and cancellation. Mapping that full lifecycle upfront let us make coherent decisions about each individual piece rather than designing flows in isolation. Every stage from the moment someone discovers SoleSavy to the moment they might decide to leave. This became the foundation for all subsequent design decisions and the shared reference point for the product and engineering teams.
section-4-1-description-2-title: Drop-off
section-4-1-description-2: What's the minimum we need to collect at sign-up to personalize the experience without creating drop-off?
section-4-1-description-3-title: Value
section-4-1-description-3: What does a new member need to do in their first session to have a reasonable chance of getting value?
section-4-1-description-4-title: Hierarchy
section-4-1-description-4: How do we sequence the onboarding steps so that each one builds on the last, rather than feeling like an arbitrary checklist? The guiding principle for both the sign-up and onboarding flows was the same - collect only what you need, when you need it, and make every step feel like progress rather than a gate.
section-4-1-description-5-title: Integrations
section-4-1-description-5: How do we integrate Chargebee's subscription management in a way that feels invisible to the member?

section-5-2-description-1-title: Onboarding
section-5-2-description-1: The onboarding redesign was the most consequential piece of the project. For the first time, SoleSavy had a structured experience that walked new members through the tools they needed to be successful — Slack, the Drops app, Collect, and the educational content that explained how releases actually worked. Each step was sequenced deliberately - lower-friction actions came first to build momentum, higher-commitment actions (like installing tools and connecting accounts) came once the member had some context for why they mattered.

section-6-1-nav: Impact
section-6-1-title: Impact & Takeaways
# section-6-1-description-1-title:
section-6-1-description-1: The rebuilt platform gave SoleSavy the infrastructure to grow in ways the Wordpress setup never could have supported — Collect, Drops, and future products were all built on top of this foundation. The sign-up flow gave the business a reliable, scalable subscription management system that didn't require agency support to maintain or extend. The onboarding redesign addressed what the data had been pointing at - new members now had a clear path from sign-up to first meaningful action, and ultimetely reducing churn.
section-6-1-description-2-title: Platform decisions are product decisions
section-6-1-description-2: Moving away from Wordpress wasn't just an engineering call — it determined what product experiences were possible. Being part of that conversation as a designer, and understanding the implications of the platform choice on the user experience, was essential to making the right call.
section-6-1-description-3-title: Map the full journey before designing any part of it
section-6-1-description-3: The sign-up and onboarding flows only made sense in the context of the member lifecycle they were part of. Designing them without that context would have produced flows that worked in isolation but failed in sequence.
section-6-1-description-4-title: Onboarding is a retention problem
section-6-1-description-4: The instinct is to think of onboarding as a welcome experience — something friendly and surface-level. The SoleSavy onboarding redesign was fundamentally about reducing early churn by getting members to value faster. That framing changed every prioritization decision.
section-6-1-description-5-title: What you collect at sign-up shapes everything downstream
section-6-1-description-5: The personalization data we gathered — sneaker sizes, brand preferences — wasn't just a nice touch. It was the input that made the rest of the product feel relevant from day one. Deciding what to ask, and when, was as important as how we asked it.
