section-1-1-nav: Overview
section-1-1-title: Overview
section-1-1-description-1: North One's envelope feature let small business owners organize their money into named buckets — a payroll envelope, a taxes envelope, an operating envelope. It was a useful concept, but the implementation had a fundamental flaw – envelopes weren't real accounts. They had no unique routing numbers, no independent locking, and when a card transaction exceeded the main account balance, everything collapsed back into a pile. For North One's most active small business customers, this was a recurring problem.
section-1-1-description-2: The solution wasn't a better envelope — it was a real multi-account experience. I co-led design on the project with Jacob Lang, working alongside product and engineering to replace the envelope system entirely.

section-2-1-nav: Context
section-2-1-title: Context
# section-2-1-description-1-title:
section-2-1-description-1: Envelopes worked as a mental model. The problem was in the mechanics. Because envelopes were folders within a single bank account — not accounts themselves — they couldn't behave the way small business owners needed them to. When a card transaction exceeded the main account balance, every envelope dumped back into the main account. This forced business owners to manually reconfigure their entire allocation — a painful, recurring process that was affecting some of North One's most active customers.
section-2-1-description-2-title: Routing Numbers
section-2-1-description-2: Envelopes had no unique routing or account numbers, making it impossible to truly separate funds by purpose. You couldn't give your payroll processor one routing number and your tax authority another.
section-2-1-description-3-title: Transfer Rules
section-2-1-description-3: Transfer rules were percentage-based and rigid, unable to accommodate the more complex financial structures these businesses required.
section-2-1-description-4-title: Reallocation Flow
section-2-1-description-4: The reallocation flow — triggered every time a transaction disrupted the balance — was one of the most complained-about experiences in the product.
# section-2-1-description-5-title:
section-2-1-description-5: The objective was clear – replace the envelope feature with a multi-account experience that resolved the allocation problem and gave North One's most valuable customers the infrastructure they actually needed.

section-3-1-nav: Research
section-3-1-title: Research
# section-3-1-description-1-title:
section-3-1-description-1: Research confirmed that the problem was more widespread than the complaint volume suggested. We already knew it was affecting top customers — but outreach confirmed that even users who hadn't been vocal about it were working around the limitations regularly.
section-3-1-description-2-title: Customer outreach
section-3-1-description-2: We reached out directly to larger, more active customers to understand how they were using envelopes and where the friction was. The feedback was consistent – the reallocation problem was a recurring frustration, and the lack of unique account numbers was a real blocker for certain workflows. Multiple customers said they'd use a multi-account feature immediately upon launch — that level of readiness made the case for the scope of the build.
section-3-1-description-3-title: Hypothesis
section-3-1-description-3: A guided experience that surfaced the highest-priority activation actions immediately after sign-up — and persisted them in an accessible, trackable format — would drive more users to the six-transaction milestone than the existing passive approach.
section-3-1-description-4-title: Competitive analysis
section-3-1-description-4: We looked at how North One's direct competitors handled account structures. True multi-account support — with independent routing numbers and separate locking — had become table stakes at the competitive tier North One was targeting. The envelope system wasn't just a UX problem; it was a product gap.
section-3-1-description-5-title: Internal audit
section-3-1-description-5: We mapped every part of the product that touched envelopes — transaction flows, transfer rules, the dashboard, account settings — to understand the full surface area of the redesign before committing to a scope.

section-4-1-nav: Ideation
section-4-1-title: Ideation
# section-4-1-description-1-title:
section-4-1-description-1: Because this feature touched nearly every part of the product, prioritization was the first and most important design problem. We couldn't redesign everything simultaneously, and the wrong sequencing would have created a confusing in-between state for users. Design led the prioritization process — working with the PM and engineering lead to rank every aspect of the feature by user impact and technical feasibility. That framework became the roadmap.
section-4-1-description-2-title: Legacy transition
section-4-1-description-2: How do we handle the transition from envelopes to accounts for existing users without breaking their current setup? 
section-4-1-description-3-title: Appwide
section-4-1-description-3: How do we represent multiple accounts on the dashboard and across the app without overwhelming users with information? How do we design the account designation step in transaction flows in a way that feels natural rather than adding friction?
section-4-1-description-4-title: Rules
section-4-1-description-4: Where do transfer rules live — and how do we make them more comprehensible without burying them in settings? How do we handle the transition from envelopes to accounts for existing users without breaking their current setup? 
section-4-1-description-5-title:
section-4-1-description-5: The transfer rules were the most complex design problem. The existing system had two rule types — one for internal transfers and one for deposits — but the new multi-account structure required more nuance. Testing revealed that the mental model was hard to follow without education, and that the frequency controls added cognitive load without meaningful value. Both informed the redesigned experience.

section-6-1-nav: Impact
section-6-1-title: Impact & Takeaways
# section-6-1-description-1-title:
section-6-1-description-1: User testing with top customers on high-fidelity designs produced exceptional reactions. Multiple small business owners proactively requested to be notified the moment the feature went live — a level of demand that validated both the problem and the solution. The business ultimately chose to prioritize acquisition and activation work over this retention-focused feature, which affected the timeline. The design position was that a fully realized multi-account experience would have meaningfully improved stickiness for North One's highest-value customers — and that tradeoff is worth naming.
section-6-1-description-2-title: Design should own prioritization for complex features
section-6-1-description-2: A feature that touches the entire product can't be scoped by any single function in isolation. Having design lead the prioritization conversation — with engineering and product as informed collaborators — produced better decisions faster than a top-down approach would have.
section-6-1-description-3-title: The most valuable users have the most complex needs
section-6-1-description-3: The customers who wanted multi-account most urgently were also the customers transacting most frequently and deriving the most value from North One. Solving for them wasn't niche — it was strategically essential.
section-6-1-description-4-title: Test the mental model, not just the interface
section-6-1-description-4: The transfer rules weren't visually broken — they were conceptually hard to follow. That distinction only became clear in testing, and it drove some of the most meaningful changes in the final design.
section-6-1-description-5-title: Transitions matter as much as destinations
section-6-1-description-5: Moving users from envelopes to accounts wasn't just a feature launch — it was a migration. Designing for that transition, rather than only for the end state, was what made the new experience viable for existing users.
