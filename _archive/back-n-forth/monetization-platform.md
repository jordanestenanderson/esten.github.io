From the very beginning — before any designs existed — I was brought in alongside the lead software engineer to help shape what the platform would actually be. section-2-1-nav: Context
section-2-1-title: Offers & Orders
# section-2-1-description-1-title:
section-2-1-description-1: The creator economy had plenty of tools for publishing content, but very little infrastructure for the transaction that happens when a creator's audience wants their work evaluated. Creators were fielding feedback requests through DMs, email, and ad-hoc tools that weren't built for the job. There was no clean way to present what you offered, collect submissions in a structured way, or deliver feedback without it becoming a logistical mess.
# section-2-1-description-2-title:
section-2-1-description-2: Back n Forth needed to solve both sides of that equation simultaneously — the creator's experience of receiving and responding to work, and the submitter's experience of getting access to someone they admired and trusted.

section-3-1-nav: Research
section-3-1-title: Research
# section-3-1-description-1-title:
section-3-1-description-1: Because we were building from scratch, our research phase was about validating the concept before committing to a direction. We spent time talking to creators across photography, design, music, and writing to understand how they were currently handling feedback requests and where the friction was.
section-3-1-description-2-title: Competitive landscape
section-3-1-description-2: Existing tools either handled the collection side (Typeform, Google Forms) or the annotation side (Loom, Markup.io) — but nothing brought them together into a single, purpose-built creator experience. That gap was the opportunity.
section-3-1-description-3-title: Interviews
section-3-1-description-3: Most creators were already getting inbound requests — they just had no good way to manage them. The feedback itself was the hard part – written notes only went so far, especially for visual work. Creators were reluctant to monetize something that felt disorganized or unprofessional.

section-4-1-nav: Ideation
section-4-1-title: Ideation
# section-4-1-description-1-title:
section-4-1-description-1: With a clear problem and a validated direction, we mapped the full creator and submitter journeys in parallel. The two experiences needed to work together — a great submitter experience doesn't matter if the creator can't manage what comes in, and a great management tool doesn't matter if nobody submits.
section-4-1-description-2-title: Multi-step form
section-4-1-description-2: How do we structure the submission form to collect enough to be useful without overwhelming someone who just wants feedback? The multi-step form was an early and important decision. A single long form killed conversion — we tested this instinct against what we knew from other creative tools and it held. Breaking the process into stages kept submitters oriented and made the ask feel manageable.

section-6-1-nav: Impact
section-6-1-title: Impact & Takeaways
# section-6-1-description-1-title:
section-6-1-description-1: Back n Forth launched to beta with a product that covered the full loop — submission, payment, feedback, delivery. For something built from a blank canvas with a small team, getting to a functional, polished MVP in scope was itself the first win. Post-launch, we ran a combination of analytics review and live user testing sessions. Watching creators use the feedback interface confirmed that the annotation tools were the feature they reached for most. The roadmap we built from those sessions prioritized expanding annotation capabilities and giving creators more control over their submission structure.
section-6-1-description-2-title: Start with the problem, not the product
section-6-1-description-2: The time we spent upfront understanding what creators actually needed — before touching any design tools — was what made the eventual design decisions feel grounded. We weren't guessing at what features to build; we had a clear rationale for each one.
section-6-1-description-3-title: Two-sided products need to be designed in parallel
section-6-1-description-3: The submitter experience and the creator experience are interdependent. Designing them separately and then trying to connect them would have created seams. Mapping both journeys together from the start kept the product coherent.
section-6-1-description-4-title: Visual feedback is a product problem, not just a feature
section-6-1-description-4: The annotation interface wasn't a detail — it was central to whether the product actually delivered on its promise. Getting that right required treating it as its own design challenge, not an afterthought on top of a text input.
section-6-1-description-5-title: Beta is the beginning of research, not the end of it
section-6-1-description-5: Launching was how we learned what we actually built. The analytics and user sessions after beta were as valuable as anything we did before it.
